\begin{abstract}
Fourier neural operators (FNOs) parameterize solution operators of partial differential equations (PDEs) in Fourier space, but standard spectral layers lack an explicit parameterization of the nonlocal, power-law dissipation characteristic of fractional differential operators $(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}$. We investigate whether incorporating learnable, channel-wise spectral filters into FNO layers provides an inductive bias for learning nonlocal dynamics. We evaluate a rational filter (fr-FNO-R), a power-law filter $\exp(-c|k|^\alpha)$, and a newly reformulated fractional Laplacian resolvent filter $(1+c|k/K|^\alpha)^{-1}$ with identity initialization (fr-FNO-L) against an expanded operator ladder: standard FNO, parameter-matched FNO, fixed Gaussian low-pass smoothing (FNO-G), and validation-tuned FNO. Across four benchmark PDEs (Space-Fractional Burgers, Fractional Allen--Cahn, Linear Fractional Diffusion, and Fractional Fisher--KPP) spanning four fractional orders $\alpha\in\{0.8, 1.2, 1.6, 1.9\}$, zero-shot super-resolution transfer across $N\in\{64, 128, 256, 512\}$, and repeated random seeds, we establish that: (1) the heuristic rational filter decays as $|k|^{-2}$ for every $\alpha$, so its parameter cannot represent fractional orders, whereas the resolvent filter strictly exhibits $\mathcal{O}(|k|^{-\alpha})$ roll-off; (2) when vanilla FNO is given equal validation hyperparameter tuning budget (Tuned-FNO), it matches or outperforms all fractional filter variants across 9 of 10 paired seed conditions ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}}=0.0156$); (3) gains on phase-field dynamics stem from generic low-pass regularization rather than learned fractional orders; and (4) unregularized Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE) diverges on continuous zero-crossing PDE solutions, motivating symmetric and $\epsilon$-regularized percentage metrics. Finally, we introduce a model-agnostic linear-response probe that recovers effective dissipation exponents with a mean absolute error of $0.072$ on linear fractional diffusion.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Neural operators learn mappings between infinite-dimensional function spaces and serve as fast, resolution-invariant surrogates for repeated numerical solves of partial differential equations (PDEs)~\cite{kovachki2023neural,lu2021learning}. The Fourier neural operator (FNO) parameterizes an integral kernel directly in the Fourier domain and maintains an efficient computational complexity of $\mathcal{O}(N\log N)$ per layer~\cite{li2021fourier,lu2022comprehensive}. In parallel, physics-informed methods embed governing equations directly into composite training losses~\cite{raissi2019physics,pang2019fpinns}.

Fractional PDEs model anomalous diffusion, non-Fickian transport, and long-range particle interactions via the fractional Laplacian operator, whose Fourier symbol is $\|k\|^{\alpha}$ for order $\alpha \in (0, 2]$~\cite{podlubny1999fractional,metzler2000random,lischke2020fractional,kwasnicki2017ten}. Standard FNO layers learn unconstrained complex weights across truncated Fourier modes without incorporating the structural power-law dissipation of nonlocal operators. A natural hypothesis is to integrate learnable fractional spectral multipliers directly into the Fourier layer~\cite{mehouachi2026fractional}.

In this paper, we present a rigorous, leak-free empirical and theoretical evaluation of adaptive fractional filtering in FNOs across four benchmark systems, four fractional orders, and four spatial resolutions. We make four primary contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Mathematical Formulation \& Reformulation:} We analyze the heuristic rational filter (fr-FNO-R) and prove that its asymptotic decay rate is fixed at $|k|^{-2}$ for all $\alpha$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:decay}). We reformulate the filter using the fractional Laplacian resolvent $(1 + c |k/K|^\alpha)^{-1}$ with identity initialization (fr-FNO-L, Proposition~\ref{prop:frac_decay}), ensuring that $\alpha$ strictly dictates the physical spectral decay rate $\mathcal{O}(|k|^{-\alpha})$.
\item \textbf{Comprehensive Operator Benchmark Ladder:} We evaluate an expanded ladder of baselines beyond vanilla FNO, including parameter-matched FNO (A1), fixed Gaussian smoothing (A2), fixed fr-FNO (A3), paper adaptive filter (A5), true fractional resolvent (A6/A6i), frozen filter controls (A7), and validation-tuned FNO.
\item \textbf{Rigorous Statistical Validation \& Parity Audit:} Over five repeated seeds, we evaluate training sizes ($N_{\mathrm{tr}} = 100$ vs. $500$) and training duration (100 vs. 300 epochs). We show that when vanilla FNO is given equal hyperparameter validation tuning, it eliminates the apparent fractional advantage ($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = 0.0156$).
\item \textbf{Resolution Generalization \& Metric Clarification:} We demonstrate zero-shot super-resolution generalization from $N=256$ to $N \in \{64, 128, 256, 512\}$. We clarify why raw MAPE is ill-posed on zero-crossing physical fields, and report Symmetric MAPE (sMAPE) and $\epsilon$-regularized MAPE. Finally, our linear-response probe recovers the ground-truth order on linear fractional diffusion with a mean absolute error (MAE) of $0.072$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Filtered Fourier Layer}
Let $v_l(x)$ denote the latent field at layer $l \in \{1, \dots, L\}$ with $C$ channels. A filtered Fourier layer computes:
\begin{equation}
v_{l+1} = \sigma\!\left(W v_l + \Fcal^{-1}\!\left(R_\phi\,\Psi\,\Fcal v_l\right)\right),
\label{eq:layer}
\end{equation}
where $W \in \mathbb{R}^{C \times C}$ is a pointwise linear map, $R_\phi(k) \in \mathbb{C}^{C \times C}$ denotes learnable spectral weights on the lowest $M$ modes, and $\Psi_i(k)$ is a channel-wise spectral attenuation filter applied to input channel $i$. Setting $\Psi \equiv 1$ recovers the standard FNO. Fig.~\ref{fig:arch} illustrates the architecture. All spectral computations use integer wavenumbers $k \in \{0, 1, \dots, M-1\}$ on the periodic domain $[0, 2\pi)$.

\subsection{Filter Families \& Mathematical Formulations}\label{sec:filters}
All filters operate channel-wise, parameterized with $\alpha_i = 2\,\mathrm{sigmoid}(\hat\alpha_i) \in (0, 2)$.

\emph{1) Heuristic Rational Filter (fr-FNO-R):}
\begin{equation}
\Psi_i(k) = \frac{1}{e^{1-\alpha_i} + \alpha_i \left(\frac{k}{M}\right)^2}.
\label{eq:rational}
\end{equation}

\emph{2) Power-Law Propagator (fr-FNO-P):}
\begin{equation}
\Psi_i(k) = \exp\!\left(-c_i\,|k|^{\alpha_i}\right),\quad c_i = \mathrm{softplus}(\hat c_i) > 0,
\label{eq:power}
\end{equation}
with $\Psi_i(0) = 1$. When $c_i = \nu T$, this corresponds to the analytical propagator for linear fractional diffusion $u_t = -\nu(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}u$ over interval $T$.

\emph{3) Reformulated Fractional Resolvent Filter (fr-FNO-L):}
To directly enforce that $\alpha$ controls the asymptotic spectral roll-off, we derive a filter from the stationary fractional resolvent equation $(I + c(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2})u = f$:
\begin{equation}
\Psi_i(k) = \frac{1}{1 + c_i \left|\frac{k}{M}\right|^{\alpha_i}},\quad c_i = \mathrm{softplus}(\hat c_i) > 0.
\label{eq:resolvent}
\end{equation}
To prevent optimization divergence and high-frequency choking, we introduce \emph{Identity Initialization} (fr-FNO-ID / Arm A6i), setting $\hat c_i$ such that $c_i \approx 10^{-3}$ and $\alpha_i \approx 1.0$, which guarantees $\Psi_i(k) \approx 1$ at step 0.

\emph{4) Gaussian Smoothing Control (FNO-G):} $\Psi(k) = \exp(-k^2 / 2\sigma^2)$ with $\sigma \in \{2, 4, 8, 16\}$ selected via validation error.

\section{Theoretical Analysis}

\begin{proposition}[Fixed Quadratic Roll-Off of Rational Filter]\label{prop:decay}
For every $\alpha > 0$, the rational filter~\eqref{eq:rational} satisfies $0 < \Psi(k) \le e^{\alpha-1}$, is monotonically decreasing in $|k|$, and satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\lim_{k \to \infty} \frac{\log \Psi(k)}{\log k} = -2.
\end{equation}
The asymptotic decay rate is $|k|^{-2}$ independent of $\alpha$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The denominator satisfies $e^{1-\alpha} + \alpha (k/M)^2 \ge e^{1-\alpha} > 0$. For $k > 0$, the derivative with respect to $k$ is $-2\alpha k M^{-2} [e^{1-\alpha} + \alpha (k/M)^2]^{-2} < 0$, establishing monotonicity. For large $k$, $\alpha (k/M)^2$ strictly dominates $e^{1-\alpha}$, yielding $\Psi(k) \sim M^2 \alpha^{-1} k^{-2}$.
\end{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:decay} demonstrates that $\alpha$ in~\eqref{eq:rational} acts primarily as a gain-scaling and knee-frequency shift parameter, but does not dictate a fractional spectral decay exponent.

\begin{proposition}[Fractional Spectral Decay of Resolvent Filter]\label{prop:frac_decay}
For any $\alpha \in (0, 2]$ and $c > 0$, the reformulated resolvent filter~\eqref{eq:resolvent} satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\lim_{k \to \infty} \frac{\log \Psi(k)}{\log k} = -\alpha.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $k \gg M$, $c (k/M)^\alpha \gg 1$. Thus, $\Psi(k) = [1 + c (k/M)^\alpha]^{-1} \sim c^{-1} M^\alpha k^{-\alpha}$. Taking logarithms yields $\log \Psi(k) = -\alpha \log k - \log c + \alpha \log M + o(1)$, which establishes the limit.
\end{proof}
Fig.~\ref{fig:decay} verifies this contrast: while the rational filter roll-off is permanently constrained to slope $-2$, the reformulated filter varies as $-\alpha$, matching the fractional Laplacian.

\begin{proposition}[Hypothesis Class Equivalence]\label{prop:absorb}
Let $\Psi_i(k) > 0$ for all $i, k$ be fixed. The set of functions realizable by layer~\eqref{eq:layer} with filter $\Psi$ is identical to the set realizable with $\Psi \equiv 1$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The filter acts via elementwise scaling on channel $i$ at mode $k$: $[R_\phi(k) \Psi(k)]_{oi} = R_{oi}(k) \Psi_i(k) = R'_{oi}(k)$. Since $\Psi_i(k) > 0$, the mapping $R_{oi}(k) \mapsto R'_{oi}(k)$ is a linear bijection on $\mathbb{C}^{C \times C}$.
\end{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:absorb} confirms that channel-wise spectral filtering alters the optimization trajectory and inductive bias, rather than enlarging the representational capacity of the network.

\section{Linear-Response Spectral Probe}
To quantify the effective spectral dissipation learned by a trained operator, we apply a linear-response probe around test inputs. For a test input $a$ and wavenumber $k$, we apply harmonic perturbations $a \mapsto a + \varepsilon \sin(kx)$ and measure the perturbation response gain:
\begin{equation}
g(k) = \left\langle \frac{\bigl|\widehat{\mathcal{G}_\Theta(a + \varepsilon\sin kx)}(k) - \widehat{\mathcal{G}_\Theta(a)}(k)\bigr|}{\varepsilon\,|\widehat{\sin kx}(k)|} \right\rangle_{a}.
\label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
Linearity is verified by confirming invariance across $\varepsilon = 10^{-3}$ and $2\times 10^{-3}$ (relative discrepancy $< 10^{-4}$). We then fit:
\begin{equation}
\log g(k) = \log C - c_{\mathrm{eff}}\,k^{\alpha_{\mathrm{eff}}}
\label{eq:fit}
\end{equation}
over modes where the product of gain and input power exceeds $10^{-3}$ of its maximum. Evaluated on the analytical propagator, the probe recovers $\alpha_{\mathrm{eff}} = \alpha_{\mathrm{true}}$, $c_{\mathrm{eff}} = 0.1000$, and $R^2 = 1.0$.

\section{Experimental Setup}

\subsection{Benchmark Datasets \& Solvers}
We evaluate models across four PDE systems on the periodic domain $[0, 2\pi)$ at native resolution $N=256$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{S1 (Space-Fractional Burgers):} $\partial_t u + u \partial_x u + \nu (-\Delta)^{\alpha/2} u = 0$, modeling nonlocal shock formation with $\nu=0.1$.
\item \textbf{S2 (Fractional Allen--Cahn):} $\partial_t u + \kappa (-\Delta)^{\alpha/2} u - u + u^3 = 0$, modeling phase-field transitions with $\kappa=0.01$.
\item \textbf{S3 (Linear Fractional Diffusion):} $\partial_t u + \nu (-\Delta)^{\alpha/2} u = 0$, solved analytically via Fourier symbol decay $\exp(-\nu |k|^\alpha T)$ with $\nu=0.1, T=1.0$.
\item \textbf{S4 (Fractional Fisher--KPP):} $\partial_t u + \kappa (-\Delta)^{\alpha/2} u - r u(1-u) = 0$, modeling front propagation with $\kappa=0.01, r=1.0$.
\end{itemize}
Initial conditions are drawn from Gaussian random fields $u_0 \sim \mathcal{GP}(0, (-\Delta + 9I)^{-2})$ and harmonic wave-packets. High-fidelity reference solutions are generated using pseudo-spectral integrating-factor ETD-RK4 solvers at $N_{\mathrm{ref}} = 512$ with $\Delta t = 5\times 10^{-4}$ and $2/3$-rule de-aliasing, achieving self-convergence errors $< 10^{-5}$.

\emph{Data Splits:} We enforce strict leak-free splits: test indices 1000--1199 (200 samples) and validation indices 900--999 (100 samples) remain fixed across all runs. Seed-dependent disjoint subsets of size $N_{\mathrm{tr}} \in \{100, 500\}$ are drawn from indices 0--899.

\subsection{Model Architecture \& Training}
All models share a common backbone: lifting layer ($1 \to 64$), four Fourier layers with width 64 and $M=16$ modes, GELU activations, and a two-layer projection ($64 \to 128 \to 1$). Models have $\sim 2.87 \times 10^5$ parameters. Optimization uses AdamW with cosine annealing over 100 epochs (extended to 300 epochs in training length ablations), batch size 50 ($N_{\mathrm{tr}}=100$) or 125 ($N_{\mathrm{tr}}=500$), and weight decay $10^{-4}$. Filter parameters use a $10\times$ learning rate multiplier without weight decay.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
The primary metric is the relative $L^2$ test error: $\|u - \hat u\|_2 / \|u\|_2$. Because continuous PDE fields exhibit zero-crossings ($u(x) = 0$), unregularized raw MAPE $\frac{100\%}{N}\sum |(u_i - \hat u_i)/u_i|$ diverges toward infinity. We therefore report:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Symmetric MAPE (sMAPE):} $\frac{100\%}{N}\sum \frac{2|u_i - \hat u_i|}{|u_i| + |\hat u_i| + 10^{-6}}$.
\item \textbf{$\epsilon$-Regularized MAPE ($\text{MAPE}_\epsilon$):} $\frac{100\%}{N}\sum \frac{|u_i - \hat u_i|}{|u_i| + 0.01\,\mathrm{std}(u)}$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Discussion \& Honest Limitations}
We distill six rigorous empirical conclusions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Inductive Bias vs. Order Learning:} On Allen--Cahn, frozen random filter A7 ($0.0112 \pm 0.0005$) matches learned filter A5 ($0.0110 \pm 0.0005$) within $1.8\%$, while fixed filter A3 ($0.0086 \pm 0.0011$) beats both, demonstrating that phase-field improvements stem from spectral low-pass prior regularization rather than fractional order discovery.
\item \textbf{Failure on Advective Shock Regimes:} On Burgers ($\alpha=0.8$), fractional filtering actively degrades accuracy; learned A5 ($0.1772$) underperforms vanilla A0 ($0.1085$) by $+63.3\%$, while additive skip architectures diverged completely ($1.0155$).
\item \textbf{Identity Initialization Eliminates Divergence but Yields Zero Nonlinear Edge:} Correcting high-frequency choking in $A_{6i}$ prevents divergence but matches vanilla A0 ($0.1085$ vs. $0.1085$ on S1 $\alpha=0.8$; $0.0549$ vs. $0.0549$ on S1 $\alpha=1.2$; $0.0256$ vs. $0.0256$ at 300 epochs), failing the pre-registered $>10\%$ threshold on 0 of 4 nonlinear benchmark cells.
\item \textbf{Tuning Parity Explains Baseline Deficits:} Providing vanilla FNO with standard validation hyperparameter tuning ($\text{lr}=3\times 10^{-3}, \text{wd}=0 / 10^{-4}$) yields $0.0389$ on Burgers and $0.0151$ on Allen--Cahn, outperforming all fractional variants across 9 of 10 paired seeds (Wilcoxon $p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = 0.0156$).
\item \textbf{Data Regime Invariance:} In small-data regimes ($N_{\mathrm{tr}}=100$), fractional operators provide no sample-efficiency gain over vanilla FNO, exhibiting identical or degraded relative errors.
\item \textbf{Probe Diagnostic Nature:} Linear response symbol probing reflects overall problem stiffness rather than governing fractional PDE orders, producing effective orders $\alpha_{\mathrm{eff}} \in [0.05, 0.53]$ on nonlinear Burgers and Allen--Cahn regardless of physical $\alpha$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
We performed a systematic theoretical and empirical evaluation of adaptive fractional Fourier neural operators across four PDEs, four fractional orders, and four spatial resolutions under a leak-free protocol. We proved that the heuristic rational filter cannot parameterize fractional decay, reformulated the filter as a true fractional Laplacian resolvent, and established that validation-tuned vanilla FNO matches or outperforms fractional variants across benchmark systems. Furthermore, our model-agnostic linear response probe recovers governing orders with an MAE of $0.072$ on linear benchmarks, providing a diagnostic tool for inspecting learned representations in neural operators.
